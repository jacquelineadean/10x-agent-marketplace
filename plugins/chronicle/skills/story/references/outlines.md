# Chapter plans

Pick the plan that fits the forum, then cut chapters the facts cannot support. Five to
seven chapters suit a status page; three suit a short update. Each line gives the chapter's
job and the blocks that usually carry it.

## Project status and history (steering committee, portfolio review)

The Keystone example follows this plan.

| Chapter | Job | Blocks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hero | State of the project in one picture and four numbers | plate with the real progress, stats |
| History | Why the project exists and how it got here | prose with dropcap, timeline |
| Foundations or achievements | What changed for the people doing the work | slope (before and after), calendar, compare, lesson |
| Status | Where things stand against plan | scrolly on the main trend, multiples for health, burnup |
| Challenges | What went wrong and what was learned | plate, stepper for the worst incident, quote, lesson, matrix of open risks |
| Outcomes and opportunities | What the work bought and what it will buy | line chart with notes, bars for cost, savings model |
| Team | Who did it and how they are organised | prose, team |
| Roadmap | What is next and what is needed | plate with spots, roadmap, forecast model, prose with the decisions asked |

## Quarterly or business review

Hero (the quarter in four numbers), Goals against results (slope or bars, one panel per
goal), What moved the numbers (scrolly), What did not work (prose, lesson), Customers or
users (multiples, quotes the user supplied), Next quarter (roadmap, decisions).

## Incident review

Hero (impact in numbers: minutes, users, errors), Timeline (stepper with the error chart),
Why it happened (prose, compare: expected versus actual), What held (lesson), Actions
(table of owners and dates, roadmap), Risks still open (matrix).

## Launch recap

Hero (adoption numbers), The road to launch (timeline), Launch day (line or calendar of
usage), What users did (multiples or bars), Lessons (lesson), What is next (roadmap).

## Research or analysis brief

Hero (the finding), The question (prose), The evidence (two or three charts, each with a
claim title), What it means (prose, compare), Caveats (prose with sources), Next steps.

## Mapping a request to blocks

| The user asks for | Use |
| --- | --- |
| status, progress, health | stats, scrolly, multiples, burnup |
| achievements, before and after | slope, compare, calendar |
| challenges, incidents | stepper, quote, lesson, matrix |
| opportunities, savings, what-if | model (savings or forecast), bars |
| history | timeline |
| roadmap, next steps | roadmap, plate with spots, forecast model |
| team, engineering org | team, stats |
